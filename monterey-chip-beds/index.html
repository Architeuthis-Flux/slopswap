Where to look first

Start with the two beds that have dated Psilocybe records and the one deep chip bed nobody has checked. Then work outward from your own three patches.

  1. CSUMB library, north-east corner, and Pinnacles Suites, west side. These hold the only precisely located Psilocybe records on the Monterey side: P. allenii on Nov 16 and Dec 2, 2025.
  2. Del Monte Ave eucalyptus grove at the Navy School gate, Monterey. Deep eucalyptus chips went down in 2024 or early 2025, and lattice stinkhorns have fruited there two summers running. It is untested for Psilocybe and the best new lead.
  3. The ground around your three patches. Try the other tees at Poppy Hills, the Custom House Plaza gardens 400 m from the Bank of America planter, and the forest tracks north of the Huckleberry Hill gate, where eight chip-fungus records sit within 1 km.
  4. George Washington Park, Pacific Grove. It has a record on wood chips and paths mapped as wood mulch.
  5. Rip Van Winkle Open Space, Forest Lodge Rd lot. Four chip-fungus records came from here in Feb 2024.
  6. Canyon Del Rey strip and Oldemeyer Center, Seaside. Street View confirms chips at both, 750 m apart.
  7. Pine Ave, Pacific Grove. P. allenii fruited here on Nov 11, 2025. Walk the public strips of the surrounding blocks.

Every spot here is pinned on the map. Details and evidence for each are in the town tables below.

What it needs

Both species feed on wood chips that have aged a season or more, stay damp, and sit in landscaping. Almost none of the records come from forest.

  • Wood chips, not soil. P. cyanescens grows on lignin-rich material, mainly chips in mulched beds (Wikipedia). For P. allenii the listed favourites are oak, eucalyptus, Douglas fir and alder mulch (Wikipedia). Stamets names alder and Douglas fir chips and sawdust (compiled notes).
  • Mixed tree-crew chips count. One Shroomery poster's best P. allenii flush came from chipped Monterey cypress mixed with eucalyptus (thread). Those are everyday Peninsula chips, so alder is not required here.
  • Bark is disputed. Wikipedia says the species typically avoids bark-based mulch. Stamets reports a fondness for decorative bark around new buildings. Treat true wood chips as the better bet and bark as worth a glance.
  • Aged, not fresh. The fungus wants wood that is "a bit more broken down", after the sugars are gone, says biologist Jason Slot in National Geographic.
  • Topped up. Beds that get fresh chips regularly gain fungi, and beds never replenished lose them (Mykoweb).
  • Wet. Alan Rockefeller's pattern in the same National Geographic piece is irrigated chips in parks, office parks and apartment complexes.
  • Edges and cover. Fruiting runs in and along the perimeter of beds. Posts in the 2023 Bay Area thread add the base of shrubs, under oak-leaf litter, and tall grass beside drying chips.
  • Man-made sites. Stamets lists courthouses, libraries, utility and police grounds, rhododendron, rose and azalea gardens, freeway plantings, nurseries, soil yards and chip piles. The 2024 Bay Area thread adds parking-lot islands, high-end housing, golf courses, shopping centres and trail edges.
  • Rarely wild. Stamets calls it virtually absent from virgin forest. One reported exception is dune grass in Humboldt County, from Alan Rockefeller in the cypress thread above.
  • Low odds per bed. Rockefeller's estimate is under 1% for a few square feet of chips and several patches per square mile of chips. Cover many beds quickly.
  • How it arrives. Airborne spores, people replanting stem bases, and possibly the mulch supply chain. National Geographic calls the supply-chain route unproven in the US.
  • A sign under the chips. Stem bases lift a clump of chips bound by tough white strands (Fungus Federation of Santa Cruz).

Timing

Expect P. allenii from mid-November and P. cyanescens from late December through February. Soaking rain sets the date. A cold night helps, but the 2025 finds came without one.

The local calendar. Public records for the Monterey Bay area hold 51 dated P. allenii and 40 dated P. cyanescens observations.

  • P. allenii: 41 of 51 fall in November and December. The earliest is mid-October and the latest mid-January.
  • P. cyanescens: 30 of 40 fall in November through January, with 7 more in February and stragglers to Mar 31.
  • On the Monterey side the hidden-location P. cyanescens records skew late: most sit between late January and mid-February.

What the weather record says. Daily lows and rain at Monterey airport, 5 km from Seaside, for Oct 1 to Nov 18 of each year:

  • In dry falls, rain sets the date. In 2018, 2023 and 2024 the first regional record came 6 to 11 days after the first quarter-inch rain.
  • Cold usually comes first. Of 62 dated records, 89% followed a night at or below 45°F within the previous ten days at the nearer airport.
  • Cold is not required when it is wet. The Pacific Grove find of Nov 11, 2025 and the CSUMB finds of Nov 16, 2025 came with no night below 46°F in the ten days before.
  • Chips dry fast in sun. Between storms, shaded and irrigated beds keep fruiting after open beds stop.

This season. Through Nov 18, 2026 the airport had no night at or below 45°F and 3.6 inches of rain. About 3 more inches fell on Nov 18 and 19. Forecast lows are 42 to 44°F for Nov 20 to 23. Start checking now and revisit each bed weekly through February.

Weather data: Iowa Environmental Mesonet, station MRY. Records: GBIF.

How to scout new beds

Scout by search image first, then let other fungi and public records confirm. A bed that grows red spy is a bed worth returning to.

  1. Copy your own patches. Your three share shade, steady moisture and wood on the ground.
    • Poppy Hills hole 9 tee: chips beside irrigated turf, under forest shade.
    • Bank of America, 200 E Franklin St: a raised brick planter under a clipped hedge on the north-facing wall. It is shaded all day.
    • Huckleberry Hill trailhead: woody debris at a forest edge where crews and walkers disturb the ground.
  2. Filter from the car. Real wood chips pass. Rock, decomposed granite, lawn and bare soil fail. Lower Broadway in Seaside fails for that reason.
  3. Walk the cool side. Check north walls, under hedges and shrubs, sprinkler overspray, bed edges against lawn or pavement, and leaf litter under deciduous trees.
  4. Favour beds one to three years old. Stamets points to plantings around newly built structures. Google Street View's "See more dates" shows when chips first appeared.
  5. Read the other mushrooms.
    • Landscaping morels in spring usually mean chips laid within the past year. Note the bed and return the next two winters.
    • Lattice stinkhorn in summer means deep, moist chips.
    • Red spy, Agrocybe putaminum, Gymnopilus aurantiophyllus and stubble rosegill in winter mean an established chip community. Red spy often grows with Psilocybe (Bay Nature) and its orange-red cap shows from a car.
    • Deadly galerina means the habitat is right and the risk is real.
  6. Mine the records. On iNaturalist, search those species in Monterey County for November to February and read the notes for the words chips or mulch. Ignore Psilocybe pins marked obscured: the real point is hidden inside a 22 km square. The map already plots 693 of these records.
  7. Watch for chip drops. Storm clean-up and fire-fuel pruning leave deep chips under eucalyptus and cypress. The Del Monte Ave grove below is one.
  8. Time the visit. Go five to ten days after a soaking rain, in the morning, and again a week later. One iNaturalist note records a find three days after rain.
  9. Keep a log. Record chip type, age, shade and result for every bed, including blanks. A patch that fruits once tends to fruit again: one iNaturalist note describes a parking lot producing both species two years running.

What a red spy sighting tells you

Red spy marks a good chip bed and often shares it with Psilocybe. It does not predict Psilocybe.

  • Psilocybe to red spy is a strong link. Of 304 open-location P. cyanescens and P. allenii records in California, 37% have red spy within 100 m. Within 250 m the share is 62%.
  • Red spy to Psilocybe is a weak link. Only 8% of 3,857 red spy records have a Psilocybe record within 100 m. Red spy is far more common.
  • By site. About a third of 164 Psilocybe sites also hold red spy. About 3% of 2,034 red spy sites hold Psilocybe.
  • Timing. Where both share a site and a season, red spy was recorded first 63% of the time, by a median of 8 days. Its season also runs later, into February and March.
  • Caveat. 85% of P. cyanescens records hide their location. The sample is mostly P. allenii, and the 8% is a floor.

Use red spy to rank beds and to time visits. A bed with red spy has suitable chips and enough moisture. Source: GBIF records for California, pulled Nov 19, 2026.

What foragers report

Shroomery holds about a dozen Monterey-area posts, and none gives a street address. They confirm both species here, the months they fruit and the kinds of site.

Monterey-area posts on Shroomery

  • Both species are here. Alan Rockefeller wrote in 2008 that P. cyanescens reaches about as far south as Monterey (post). P. allenii from Monterey Bay was posted in 2008, 2010 and 2012.
  • A forest patch on the Peninsula. In March 2020 a Peninsula poster picked about 40 P. cyanescens in a month from one forest spot (post). They had searched the Peninsula's forests and found no second spot. Your Huckleberry Hill patch is the same kind of site.
  • The season runs from early November to late March. A Monterey Bay patch flushed by Nov 7, 2008, right after rain. A Monterey patch was picked on Nov 13, 2007 (post). The forest patch above still fruited on Mar 30, 2020.
  • Carmel-by-the-Sea. P. allenii fruited at the front door of a holiday rental on Jan 2, 2022 (post).
  • South Monterey County. P. allenii filled a restaurant garden on a main street on Nov 22, 2019, confirmed by Alan Rockefeller (thread). The bed sat in the building's shade under roses, and its soil was still wet. Every other chip bed the poster checked in town was dry and empty.
  • Salinas and Watsonville. A former Monterey resident recalled in 2015 that both species had turned up in Watsonville, and probably Salinas (post). That is the only forum support for your Watsonville report.
  • Scout while it is dry. The same poster walks chip beds in dry weather and notes the few that stay moist. Those beds fruit first when rain comes.
  • Site types. Their list for a Monterey beginner: office and apartment complexes, hotels, libraries, city parks, fire stations, schools and fast-food restaurants (post). Check right against the plants in each bed.
  • Chipped Monterey pine. A January 2025 post from the central coast describes P. allenii in landscaping chips, then more in nearby grass (post). Chipped Monterey pines had been spread across that yard.
  • Dry patches come back. Posters in the 2019 thread say the mycelium goes dormant through a dry summer and fruits again with moisture and cold. Irrigation matters most in dry years.

Bay Area threads and other sources

  • Cypress and eucalyptus chips work. A Shroomery poster's biggest P. allenii flush grew under Monterey cypress in chipped cypress and eucalyptus. They planned to search Monterey's native cypress groves next (2019 thread).
  • Monterey runs late. A Reddit commenter in Sep 2024 envied far-northern California for starting much earlier than "the spots around Monterey and SF" (r/druggardening).
  • Rain, then a small cool-down. In the 2024 Bay Area thread the ground was dust-dry on Nov 1. By Nov 13 one poster credited a few decent rains over three to four weeks for pinning, and two days later a minor temperature change plus moisture.
  • Mid-November is the usual start. That thread's first P. allenii came Nov 16 and its first P. cyanescens pin Nov 17. Another poster's first patch the year before came Nov 18.
  • Irrigation beats weather. Irrigated chips were producing while San Francisco and the East Bay were still dry.
  • Where in the bed. Posts in the 2023 thread describe one P. allenii at the base of hydrangeas, more thriving in shaggy grass while the chips dried out, and P. cyanescens mostly buried under oak leaves.
  • Site types. The 2024 thread names parking lots, high-end housing, golf courses, shopping centres and trails with foot traffic. iNaturalist notes add a Pacifica parking lot and a bed of ferns and lavender.
  • Dune grass and lupine. Posters report finds among dune grasses and lupins on the north coast, and Alan Rockefeller found P. allenii in dune grass in Humboldt County. Nobody has reported that here. Asilomar and Fort Ord Dunes have the habitat, and both are state parks where collecting is not allowed.
  • People seed patches. Posters describe placing stem butts and spawn in beds, and Rockefeller replants stem bases in fresh chips nearby. A productive bed is likely to have neighbours within walking distance.
  • Crowds find the obvious beds. Rockefeller describes hundreds of people checking Golden Gate Park's chips each December (Wikipedia). Monterey likely has far fewer hunters, so many beds here go unchecked.

Telling it apart

Every mushroom needs two checks before anything else: it bruises blue, and it drops a dark purple-brown spore print. A rusty print means stop.

Descriptions follow the Fungus Federation of Santa Cruz and Wikipedia on P. allenii.

  • The two Psilocybes cannot be separated under a microscope. The wavy edge is the field difference, and young caps of both look alike.
  • Two more rusty-spored look-alikes share the beds: Pholiotina and the Conocybe tenera group. Both have fragile stems and no strong bluing.
  • Deadly galerina fruits in the same months. Of 98 regional records, 44 are from December and 36 from January.
  • It is already at your spots. It has been recorded at four pinned sites on the Santa Cruz side, and a Galerina species was logged four times within 100 m of the CSUMB library in January 2024.
  • Check every mushroom, not every patch. Mixed clusters are the usual way people get hurt.

Law, access and limits

Nothing in this guide was checked on the ground. Treat every lead as a place to look, not a promise.

Law and access

  • Possessing psilocybin mushrooms is illegal under California and federal law. Santa Cruz's 2020 low-priority resolution covers that city only.
  • Poppy Hills is a private course in play. The Pebble Beach gates charge non-residents to enter.
  • State parks, state historic parks and reserves do not allow collecting.
  • Several records sit on private frontages. Those rows say so.

What this guide could not verify

  • Chip type. Street View separates chips from bark, rock and lawn. It cannot show the wood species, so hardwood is unconfirmed everywhere.
  • Imagery age. Street View dates are Aug to Sep 2024, and 2008 at the Del Monte Ave grove.
  • The Watsonville report. No street-level source turned up.
  • Hidden-location records. They cannot be placed closer than a 22 km square, and this guide does not try to work around that.
  • Forum search. Shroomery's own search was run signed in for Monterey, Pacific Grove, Pebble Beach, Carmel, Salinas, Watsonville, King City and Fort Ord. Other forums were read only through public search.
  • Indicator fungi. They show an established chip bed, not Psilocybe.
