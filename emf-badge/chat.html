Claude

The page now has a "Part 1, continued" section with three new schematics (Options D, E and F), each with parts and prices.

The simple knob: Option E. A pot that sets the frequency shift is what the chopper mixer (Option C) already is, and it keeps loudness and texture because mixing is linear. Option E is the same two chips (74HC4066 + 74HC14) with two changes so one pot covers the whole band: - Lower oscillator range: about 18–97 kHz, with its harmonics converting the higher bands (a 50 kHz setting also picks up fields near 150 and 250 kHz, more weakly). - Three-position mode switch: NORMAL, TUNED and COMB. COMB drives the switches with a ~3 µs pulse, so the whole band folds into hearing at once and the pot becomes a pitch trim. The cost is level, about −20 dB in that mode.

This is the one I'd build first.

Loudness on the dividers: Option D. One more 74HC4066 (two of its four switches) between the divider and channel B. The first switch rectifies the ultrasonic signal in step with itself, the second switches it at the divided frequency, so the divided tone's height follows field strength. It replaces the fixed-level injection in Option A or B.

Automatic with a slow glide: Option F. A diode-pump frequency-to-voltage stage steers the audio oscillator in a 74HC4046 ($1.33), which replaces the divider tap in Option D. Any source from about 20 kHz to 1 MHz lands within a couple of octaves, higher sources still give higher notes, and one capacitor sets the glide (about 40 ms with 1 µF). Two cautions: - It is the least proven circuit on the page: the values are scaled from one datasheet point and it hasn't been built. - The 74HC4046 oscillator needs 3 V or more, so it won't run from the two AAAs.

The page also describes two other automatic routes in text only, not drawn or priced: a stepping auto-ranger (three more logic chips, pitch arrives in octave jumps) and a small microcontroller that measures the frequency and steers the chopper.

Dividing and mixing sound different: dividing keeps frequency ratios and hears the whole band, mixing keeps frequency differences and zooms in on texture. That is why I'd still breadboard a divider next to Option E before settling.

The guide and emf_chopper.py are ready. The script has not run on a real Jumperless: I checked every call against the JumperlOS 5.7.11.5 source and ran the control logic against a simulated board only.

  • What you build: one N-MOSFET, a 1 µF capacitor and a 100 kΩ resistor on the breadboard, with three clip leads to the badge. The Jumperless drives the MOSFET gate as the chopper's oscillator.
  • What you can hear: the badge as normal, the tuned chopper, comb mode with adjustable pulse width, an automatic sweep, and a seek mode that parks on the strongest source. A real pot can also stand in for the dial so you can try tapers.
  • Your CD4049: two inverters square the signal up for the Jumperless, which adds the dividers (Options A and B) and a tracking mode that glides any source to a steady note. Three spare inverters make a real RC oscillator if you want the true analog knob.
  • Listening: headphones on the badge are best. The buffer-to-speaker and USB microphone paths are both in the script. USB capture is coarse, about 4.5 mV per step, so it suits strong sources.
  • Least certain: the PIO counter and divider behind the CD4049 modes, and whether a 2N7000 turns on hard enough from 3.3 V. The script switches those modes off or falls back by itself if the firmware refuses, and the guide lists the rest.

The bodge differs from the real 74HC4066 circuit in three audible ways: more hiss, bass leaking through unchopped, and stepped tuning. The guide covers these.

If the drawer also holds a CD4066, CD4016 or CD4053, or a CD4040 or CD4060, tell me. Those are the real switch and divider, and I would rework the hookup around them.

The generator is live at https://slopswap.org/emf-badge/kicad.html, with the add-on board proposal on the same page and pot sketches on the pots page. The deploy succeeded and the live page and picture are being served.

The generator

  • Options: switch chip or transistor build, through-the-chip or true bypass, comb mode, 9 mm shaft pot or trimmer, additions only or with the badge circuit. That makes twenty files.
  • Connections: KiCad 9.0.9 exported a netlist for each of the twenty, and each matches a netlist I wrote by hand. The browser downloads are byte-identical to those checked files.
  • Rule check: with the badge on the sheet there are no errors. Additions-only sheets report undriven power rails and dangling labels until pasted into the badge schematic.
  • Footprints: every added part's footprint exists in KiCad's library with pads matching the symbol's pins. All have a 3D model except the two pots, for which KiCad ships none.
  • LCSC numbers: in an LCSC field, with the maker's number in MPN. Chips, pot, switches and resistors are filled in; capacitors are blank because the right one depends on lead pitch.

Where it differs from what you described

  • True bypass is a two-pole switch that replaces the mode switch and takes each coil straight to its resistor. The oscillator keeps running in NORMAL, because stopping it needs a third pole and KiCad has no three-pole slide footprint. I expect only ultrasonic ripple, but that is not simulated.
  • Comb mode uses a second two-position switch, not one three-position switch. KiCad's only three-position slide footprint has no 3D model, I could not confirm its pin order, and a search did not find it at LCSC.
  • The main page was wrong to say one extra pole gives a bypass; it needs two. That is corrected.
  • The badge's schematic is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The with-badge sheet redraws it with attribution to the repo, not by name.

The add-on board

It is a real KiCad board with parts placed but not routed, rendered in 3D on a copy of the badge's own board file with R1 and R2 removed. It stands on four pins in the resistor holes, between the battery holders, and is 29.8 × 37 mm.

I read "follow the angled outline and sit above the coils" as lower edges parallel to the badge's bottom edges, stopping just short of the coil pads. If you meant it to cover the coils, the outline needs redrawing.

Open problems: - It needs three wires for power. - It comes within a fraction of a millimetre of the battery holders. - The pot in the picture is a stand-in block.

The placement-only board file is linked from the page.

Not verified

  • Nothing has been built or laid out.
  • The two-pole switch's throw pairing (2-1 with 5-6) is read from pad positions; check it with a meter.
  • The pot footprint is drawn for the PTV09A-1 and the LCSC part is a -4. The datasheet text suggests one shared board pattern, but I could not see the drawing, nor which end is clockwise.
  • Resistor LCSC numbers come from listing titles; the 2.2 kΩ one (C58595) is inferred from its neighbours in the series.

All three are done and live: the generator now opens with comb mode included and the trimmer selected, and the add-on render shows the coils as inductors.

Leaving out the 100 Ω resistors

Yes, in simulation the chip's own resistance does the job.

  • Tuned and comb: nothing changes. The tuned note goes from 59 to 61 mV, and the comb and the hiss are the same within run-to-run noise.
  • Normal listening through the chip: it gets closer to stock, within 0.5 dB when the chip is 120 Ω, where it was 2.5 dB down with the resistor.
  • The catch: the level then follows the chip's resistance, which the datasheet does not pin down at 3 V. I get +0.7 dB at 70 Ω, −2 dB at 200 Ω and −5 dB at 400 Ω, which is where tired cells may put it.
  • Output offset: it grows, most at the top of the dial: 0.34 V at 800 kHz against 0.20 V with the resistor.
  • Bypass switch: the two do not go together. A bypass would then join the coil straight to the op-amp and lift everything below 300 Hz by 2 to 3 dB.

These figures assume a 250 Ω coil; the datasheet maximum is 300 Ω. I added this to the main page's cost-cutting list with its script, badge_noresistor.py. I have not made it a generator option or taken the resistors off the add-on; say if you want either.

A correction to the main page

Checking this turned up a mistake of mine. The output offsets I quoted for the top of the dial came from runs with a series capacitor that the build does not have. Without it the simulated offset is 0.16 V at 600 kHz and about 0.24 V at 1 MHz, not 0.1 V. The tone figures are unaffected. The page and badge_chop.py are fixed.

The coils

They are now drawn as Bourns RLB1014 inductors, the part the Make article's parts list names: 8.7 mm across and 12 mm long, from the datasheet. I drew them bent over so the body lies just past the board edge, which is my reading of the footprint and not taken from a photo of a built badge. If the kit mounts them differently, tell me and I will re-pose them. The old pictures may linger for a few minutes in the site's cache.

The add-on picture still shows the base build with a shaft pot, not the new default of trimmer plus comb.
