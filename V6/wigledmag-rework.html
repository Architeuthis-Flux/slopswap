The 360 hole LEDs are one continuous 30 × 12 grid at 2.54 mm in both directions, and each LED's four pads are a square pattern. So a square block of LEDs lifted and turned a quarter turn about its own centre lands every LED back on a set of pads, each LED now turned the way it should be. The rails are 2 × 30 strips at the same pitch: a strip turned a half turn also lands back on itself.

360 颗孔位灯珠是一整块 30 × 12 的正方格，横竖间距都是 2.54 mm，每颗灯珠的 4 个焊盘也是正方形排列。所以把一块正方形的灯珠整体拿起来、绕自己的中心转 90° 再放回去，每一颗都会落在一组焊盘上，而且方向正好改过来了。电源轨是同样间距的 2 × 30 一条：整条转 180° 也会落回原位。

Notes for Kevin (not for the shop)给 Kevin 的备注（不用给师傅看）

Why pad 21, and the host side after the rework

  • SD0 → pad 21 (PC4) instead of pad 22 (PC5): pad 21 is the A2 net, which only goes to J12 pin 21 and TP5, so it needs no cut; pad 22 is the SCL_0 stub to R1 and would need one. PC5 then just sits on the host SCL as an input. Firmware: SD lines PC4/PA5/PA7/PB6/PB3/PB1/PC0/PC2, SC lines PA4/PA6/PB7/PB5/PB2/PB0/PC1/PC3 (channel 0‑7 in that order), all readable in three VPORT reads.
  • J12 pins 19/20/21 must no longer be grounded at the host (they were the A0–A2 ground wires). 19 = host TX → PA2 (USART0 alternate RXD, works on 0/1/2‑series), 20 = host RX ← PA3 (bit‑banged TX on every part: the hardware TXD options are PA1 — pad 24, tied to 3V3 — and PB2/PC2, which are channel lines; the 2‑series USART1 adds nothing free). 21 carries SD0 now: leave it open. 22 is floating after the pad‑3 cut: leave it open, or stop driving GP2.
  • UPDI = pad 23 = SDA_0. Program through TP9 (J12 end) or TP15 (J13 end) with the host's SDA pin tri‑stated and the TMAG supplies off. Bus traffic can enable the UPDI PHY now and then; a valid frame by accident is very unlikely, and the PHY times out, but if a tiny ever resets under heavy bus traffic, cut the pad‑23 stub (the SDA_0 trace leaving pad 23 toward R2) and program on the pad. Pre‑programming the chips on a QFN‑24 socket before the shop solders them avoids the question entirely.
  • No pull‑ups on the 16 channel lines (R1/R2 are on the host bus only). Drive SCL push‑pull and SDA open‑drain with the internal pull‑up (≈35 k); traces are short.
  • There is no capacitor at all on +3.3V on the board (U3's output has none), hence the 100 nF on the chip. Once the LEDs are fixed, LED_5V is live again and U3 makes 3.3 V: remove the host 3V3 feed from pin 16 or you have two regulators fighting. A 1–4.7 µF across TP11/TP12 (J13 end) would not hurt.
  • Exposed pad: the footprint's centre pad has no net and the tinyAVR's pad is not electrically connected (datasheet 4.1.1), so leaving it floating is fine; it only loses a thermal path the chip does not need.
  • TMAG edge pins: new map MVCC_3 M58→J12·23, MVCC_4 M51→J13·25, MVCC_5 M53→J13·23, MVCC_6 M55→J12·30, MVCC_7 M57→J13·26, MVCC_8 M59→J13·24; MGND_0–2 stay on J13·20–22 and still need GND at the host. Your V‑next schematic puts MVCC_3 on J13·7 and MVCC_4–8 on J13·20–24; matching that on these boards would mean freeing 20–22 (3 more cuts + 3 ground wires), which I left out. If you want it, say so and I will redraw. BoardPins.h needs the new assignments either way.
  • Rail LEDs: in your photo the top‑rail parts sit the same way as the hole parts (mark toward the top edge), while the design wants their mark toward the bottom edge — 180° off, not 90°. Check one before the shop starts; it decides whether the rails go as 2×30 strips (180°) or 2×2 blocks (90°).
  • Block flip, the mechanics: the hole LEDs are one 30×12 grid at 2.54 mm (the "gap" between the two bands is exactly one pitch), pads are a square pattern, so any square block turned about its centre lands on pads. Carrier: a flat plate (glass slide, aluminium, FR4 offcut) faced with polyimide tape, pressed onto the block; board on a hot plate ~230–240 °C or a preheater at ~160 °C plus hot air over the block; lift when every joint in the block is molten, turn, set back onto fluxed pads while they are still molten or reflow once more; peel the tape cold. Datasheet allows two reflows and they have had one — a lift‑and‑return done within one heat cycle keeps it at two. The 2 V / 3.5 A episode was ~15 mW per LED through ESD diodes; I would expect them to survive, and a dead one shows up at once because the chain stops there. Bake the boards (60 °C, 24 h) before any of this: MSL 5, unbagged since September.
  • Simulated check: with exactly the six cuts drawn, the copper graph splits into the intended islands — MVCC_0: {M52, J13·4} {M55, J12·30, TP17} {M58}; MVCC_1: {M54, J13·5, J12·31, TP18} {M57} {M51}; MVCC_2: {M56, J13·6, J12·32, TP19} {M53} {M59}. TP17 stays with M55 (handy probe point), TP18/TP19 with the top row.

Sourcing

  • ATtiny3217‑MFR is LCSC C617969; ATtiny1627/3227 (‑MU/‑MFR), 1617, 817, 807/1607/3207 are equivalent for this job — the package suffix must be the 24‑pin VQFN ("M" in Microchip's code). In HQB ask for 「ATtiny3217 QFN24」 or 「ATtiny1616 的 24 脚版」; 1616/3216 are 20‑pin and do not fit.
  • LEDs: XL‑1010RGBC‑WS2812B, LCSC C5349953, ≈ $0.05–0.08 each, 4 000 per reel; two reels. XINGLIGHT is a Shenzhen brand and HQB LED counters stock the 1010 WS2812 family — bring the exact part number so the pad pattern and pinout match.
  • Enamel wire 0.1–0.15 mm (漆包线), UV solder mask (UV 阻焊油/绿油), 100 nF 0201 — any tools counter.

Finding the right person in Huaqiangbei在华强北找对人

Two different trades do this work, and they sit in different buildings. Floors and booths move, so treat the building notes as where to start walking, and the signage and bench checks as the real test.

Trade 1 — microsoldering benches (U1, the sensor wires, or hand‑turning LEDs)

Who they are. Phone‑repair technicians. The ones to look for are the counters advertising iPhone 内存扩容 / 硬盘升级 (storage upgrades): that job is a BGA NAND swap under a microscope every day, so they have a preheater, a temperature‑controlled hot‑air station, flux, enamel wire and a UV lamp — exactly the kit for 0.5 mm QFN work and 飞线. Signs to read: 主板维修 · 芯片级维修 · BGA返修 / 植锡 · 飞线 · 内存扩容.

Where. The phone‑repair floors: the ground floor and the communications‑market side of 赛格广场 (SEG Plaza, 华强北路 1002 号), 通天地通讯城, the repair floors of 远望数码城, 飞扬时代大厦 and 龙胜. All within a ten‑minute walk of each other along 华强北路 / 振兴路.

What to look for at the bench. A stereo microscope that is actually switched on; a preheater plate (预热台) under the board they are working on; an ESD mat and strap; a tray of enamel wire spools and a UV curing lamp — if those two are on the bench, they do fly‑wire repairs routinely. A bench with only a hot‑air gun and an iron, no preheater, is a bad fit for this board: it is a thick four‑layer panel with a lot of copper, and hot air alone lifts pads before it melts solder.

How to pick one. Show the Chinese card, then Fig. 2 and Fig. 4 on your phone. Ask them to point at pad 3 and pad 21 on your actual board. If they find them in a few seconds and reach for the microscope, you are in the right place; if they wave it off as easy without looking, keep walking. Then: 「先做一块样」 (one board first), check it with a meter against the lists on the bodges tab, then hand over the rest.

Trade 2 — only if you strip and replace instead

With the block method, the LED job is a repair‑bench job too: a hot plate or preheater under the board, a flat carrier with polyimide tape over a block, lift while molten, quarter turn, set back on fluxed pads. Same benches as Trade 1; ask 「有没有加热台 / 预热台」 and whether they have done 整块转移 before. Only if you decide to strip and fit new LEDs does it become a small‑batch SMT job (stencil, pick‑and‑place, oven), which is not a market stall: those are the SMT 打样 / 小批量贴片 houses in 龙华, 宝安, 龙岗, reachable via 淘宝 / 1688 / 闲鱼 (search SMT打样 深圳 来料加工 返修) or through the 钢网 (stencil) counters on the upper floors of 赛格电子市场 and 华强电子世界, who supply all of them. Turnaround there is 2–5 days.

Things to watch out for

  • Bake first. The LEDs are MSL 5 and have been on the boards unbagged since September; reheating them without a 60 °C / 24 h bake risks popped lenses. A shop that has never heard of baking is not the shop for this.
  • Carrier, not tweezers. The point of the block method is that a block moves as one rigid piece on a flat carrier. Someone proposing to nudge 480 parts one at a time with tweezers has not understood it, and will charge per part.
  • Orientation. Make them show you, on your board, which way the green mark goes for a hole LED and for a rail LED before they start. The two groups differ; a shop that turns everything by the same angle gets 120 of them wrong.
  • The back side. Say explicitly that the nine SOT‑23‑6 sensors, the small WLP parts and the QFN stay. Some shops “tidy up” anything that looks loose.
  • Order of work. LEDs first (the board gets hot), then any wires. If you have already bodged a board, keep it out of the batch.
  • Deposit and proof. Pay a deposit (定金) for the sample board only, photograph your boards before handing them over, get the booth number (档口号) and a WeChat contact, and agree on what “done” means in writing on WeChat — the acceptance lines on the LED tab are enough.

Practicalities

  • Hours: roughly 10:00–18:30; the phone‑repair floors run later than the component floors. Mid‑afternoon is the easiest time to get someone’s attention.
  • Payment: WeChat Pay or Alipay everywhere (foreign cards work in both); small jobs are quoted and paid on the spot, SMT jobs on delivery.
  • Language: expect Chinese only. The card, the drawings and WeChat’s built‑in translate are enough; vendors are used to it.
  • Prices, order of magnitude only: bodge work tens of yuan per board per job at a repair bench (budget ¥500–2 000 for both jobs on ten boards); the LED block flip is six lifts per board, so expect a per‑board price in the same range once they have done the first one; the strip‑and‑replace route is ¥100–300 for a stencil plus low hundreds per board at a small SMT shop. A quote several times these is a “go away” price; the next counter is ten metres on.
