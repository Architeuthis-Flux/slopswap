Edge connector options for V6 modules

Every way found so far to join a Jumperless module to the main board along an edge, with what each costs in board area, height and money: one 70-way connector, or two or three smaller ones so half a breadboard can be swapped for something else. Catalog parts, Chinese and Taiwanese makers that are not (or barely) on LCSC, the custom "kiss slot" coupler, SFP-style right-angle sockets, the single-contact SMT plug-and-socket clips, and the shorted-when-empty type. Each coplanar and right-angle part can be seen on a real wig and barnacle in the 3D viewer. Vertical-mount parts are kept in the data but hidden unless the geometry filter asks for them. Dimensions are read from the makers' drawings and prices from listings as of 22 Nov 2026; anything marked ≈ is an estimate. Yuan prices are converted at ¥6.7 to the dollar.

Dead area is PCB that can hold nothing else. On a coplanar joint it is connector length × the whole strip from the back of the host pads to the tip of the module's tongue (host depth + board gap + tongue). On a vertical or raised joint it is the connector's footprint on the host plus the module's tongue. Host depth is solder legs on a straddle part, body plus about 1.8 mm of SMT leads on a vertical one, and body plus tails on a right-angle one. Gap is the distance from the host's edge to the tip of the card on a coplanar joint; tongue is the depth of the module the connector takes. Z height is how far the part stands off the board: total thickness for a coplanar part. Edge used allows 2 mm between connectors and turns orange when it exceeds the edge available. The signal count is a minimum: a connector with more contacts than asked for is fine and its extras are listed as spare, and edge holds is the most contacts that option could put on the edge with that many connectors. Depth reads host + gap + tongue for a coplanar part and host + tongue otherwise. A fixed-size part (PCIe, M.2) uses as many connectors as the signal count needs. Price is the listed price of the nearest size, scaled by contacts0.75 to the size used. What the numbers say

  • At 1.27 mm a card edge will not split. Two 36-way housings need 59.9 mm and three 24-ways 68 mm, against the 50.5 mm one 70-way uses, because every housing brings its own 7.4 mm of end walls. A custom housing with one divider wall should carry 2 × 34 contacts in the same 50.5 mm (an estimate: 1.2 mm walls, today's 2.3 mm tongue margin).
  • Two connectors on a 51 mm edge, from stock. Coplanar: Samtec MEC8-EM (2 × 40 in 49.6 mm, about $7.80 the pair) or the 1.27 mm header pair (2 × 36 in 48.5 mm, $0.58). Right angle: Samtec MEC6-RA or MEC8-RA, two TE Sliver 56-ways (46.7 mm), two M.2 sockets, or four SFP sockets. Three only fit as 1.27 mm header pairs (3 × 24 in 50.9 mm), four SFP sockets, or Oupiin's 0.635 mm right-angle part.
  • Coplanar from stock means a straddle part: a 12 to 14.5 mm strip with the boards about 2 mm apart at best, against 18.3 mm and 6.4 mm today. The XKB PCIe straddle is $0.57 but its screw ears make it 34.8 mm long.
  • The kiss slot is still the only card-edge option with the boards touching and a strip under 10 mm, and it is 5 mm thick where the straddle parts are 7 to 9.
  • SFP-style for a 1.6 mm card is the SFF-TA-1002 family (TE Sliver, Amphenol Mini Cool Edge): a right-angle 56-way is 22.3 × 11.1 × 5.35 mm, and there is a straddle version. It costs $4.40 to $5.80 a piece in reel quantities with a 12 to 16 week lead, and puts 0.6 mm fingers on the barnacle. More under SFP-style parts.
  • 1.27 mm pin headers are the cheap coplanar answer if the barnacle may carry a part. A right-angle socket on the wig and a right-angle pin header on the barnacle put 78 contacts on the edge for about $0.50 with the boards touching, 3.4 mm tall on one face and through holes in both boards. Straddle-mounting the same headers would stand only 0.9 mm proud of each face with no holes, but catalogue tails fit 0.8 to 1.0 mm boards, not 1.4 or 1.6: that version needs tails formed to order. Sections under pin headers. 2.54 mm fits only 38.
  • Cheapest per contact is M.2: 67 contacts for $0.18, if a 0.8 mm card raised above the host at 0.5 A a contact is acceptable.

The options, one by one

    SFP-style sockets and straddle parts for a 1.6 mm card

    An SFP socket is a small right-angle SMT part that a module card slides into, parallel to the host and a couple of millimetres above it. The real one is 20 contacts at 0.8 mm in 11.0 × 9.4 × 5.2 mm and costs $0.29 (HCTL HC-SFP-20P at LCSC), but its card is 1.0 mm thick. Parts built the same way for a 1.57 to 1.6 mm card:

    1.27 mm pin headers: right angle, or straddle mounted

    Three sections through one pin, to scale, with the 1.4 mm wig on the left and the 1.6 mm barnacle on the right. Dimensions are from the makers' drawings: XKB X1321FR (right-angle socket), Kinghelm KH-1.27PH90 and XFCN PZ127V (pin headers), Antenk FHCU01 (straddle socket). The same parts are modelled pin by pin in the 3D viewer: right angle, straddle (untick "tails formed to the boards" there to see B).

    Can they be straddle mounted? Not on 1.4 or 1.6 mm boards with catalogue parts. The two rows are 1.27 mm apart whatever the maker, so a pin header with 0.4 mm square pins has 0.87 mm between its rows, and the one drawing of a straddle (夹板) socket I could get at this pitch, Antenk's, shows its tails pinched to 0.50 mm. As they come, these suit boards of 0.8 to 1.0 mm at most. A straight 2.54 mm header has 1.9 mm between its pins, which is why straddling a 1.6 mm board is routine at that pitch and not at this one.

    What would make the straddle version real:

    • Ask for formed tails. The socket's tails have to end up 1.4 mm apart instead of 0.50, and each row of the pin header has to be bent out 0.37 mm to clear a 1.6 mm board. Straddle sockets are sold to order rather than off a shelf (Antenk FHCU01, 2 to 100 contacts, minimum order 1,000; Yonglifeng, from 10,000; Huifeng 回峰 lists an H = 4.4 one), and the same style at 2.54 mm is drawn with its tails formed to 1.00 mm, so the gap looks like a parameter. I found no listing for 1.4 or 1.6 mm at 1.27 pitch, and none for a straddle pin header at this pitch, so both need a drawing and a quote.
    • Or thin the barnacle. A 0.8 mm barnacle takes a stock straight pin header as it is (0.87 mm between the pins). The wig is still 1.4 mm, so its socket still needs formed tails.
    • What a straddle joint lacks: nothing grips the boards but the solder. Every push and pull goes through the pads in shear, where a card-edge straddle connector has moulded legs or a slot. With pads on both faces that is probably fine, but it wants a pull test before it is trusted.
    • Two leads not yet checked against a drawing: headers with 1.27 mm pitch and 2.54 mm between the rows (Antenk lists pin headers and sockets), whose pins would stand about 2.1 mm apart, loose on 1.6 mm and right for 2.0 mm; and horizontal surface-mount (卧贴) 1.27 mm pairs, which keep everything on one face like A but without the holes.

    Printing the pinout on the connector

    Labelled headers already exist as products, so this is a known job for a header factory or a small print shop: Arduino sells printed MKR headers ($1.50 for a 14-way strip, retail), and Where Labs sells Pico headers with a two-layer black-and-white silkscreen, straight or right-angle, at $4.95 a pair retail, printed for them by a small silkscreen shop.     What fits. A right-angle 1.27 mm socket shows a top face 4.3 mm deep with 1.27 mm of width per pin, so each label has to run front to back at about 0.9 mm character height, and the two rows share that strip: roughly two or three characters per pin. That is laser territory. A 2.54 mm socket has a top face 8.5 mm deep and twice the width per pin, which takes four or five characters per pin by pad printing. Stock catalog connectors (Samtec, Oupiin, the M.2 and PCIe sockets) can be marked the same way after purchase, but SMT parts come on tape and reel and would have to be unreeled, marked and re-reeled, which costs more than the marking; through-hole headers in tubes or bags do not have that problem. Expect a shop to add a minimum job charge and a fixture for parts this small.

    Single-contact SMT plug and socket (the Keystone 6100 / MAC8 KO-KC style)

    A stamped tab soldered flat on one board slides into a stamped spring socket soldered flat on the other, joining two boards in the same plane with nothing but metal: about 1.2 mm tall, on one face only, with the boards almost touching. Keystone and MAC8 make the gold-plated originals. The Chinese LED-lighting trade makes the same thing in tin-plated phosphor copper for joining light-bar boards end to end, at roughly a tenth of the price. Both boards need a part soldered on, so the module is no longer a bare PCB, and at about 3 mm per contact a 51 mm edge holds 16 or 17 of them per face.

    The custom "kiss slot" coupler

    The custom part designed for V6 (write-ups: V6_edge_coupler_connector_design and V6_edge_coupler_v2_kiss_slot in the project). Same 1.27 mm, 70-way, 45.47 mm tongue as today's board, but half the length end to end, 5.0 mm thick instead of 8.9, and the two boards touch inside the housing. The same contact strip in a shorter housing gives the 2-way and 3-way versions in the table above.

    Models: all five variants side by side (STEP, 7 MB zip): today's WingTAT with cards, the 2.4 mm-web version, the kiss slot, a 7.8 mm short kiss slot and the 0.8 mm sister housing. Estimated cost at 10 k a year: $2.7–4.2 in BeCu, $1.5–2.6 in phosphor bronze (which needs about 0.5 mm more height), against $2.15 for the WingTAT; tooling $13–25 k. The point is size and the touching boards, not price.

    Shorted-when-empty ("normalling") connectors

    A card-edge connector whose two rows of contacts touch each other when the slot is empty and are spread onto the two faces of the card when one is inserted. It exists as a technique (telecom DSX patch bays call it normalling, the patents say make-before-break or switching contacts) but not as a current catalog part at any fine pitch. Everything that covered it has expired:

    US 4,087,151Magnetic Controls / ADC, 1977second "normalling" hump on each contact rests against its opposite number; the card touches the first hump before spreading the second (make-before-break)
    US 4,514,030Methode, 1983three contact points per beam, the innermost shorts to the opposing contact
    US 5,098,306Burndy, 1992card edge connector with switching contacts
    US 5,286,215ADC Telecommunications, 1992normally-closed arms plus separate normally-open entrance springs
    US 6,764,345Tyco, 2003dual shorting contacts in BeCu, mate-before-break

    The only card-edge part with evidence of doing it is vintage: a synth-diy thread about a Buchla 208 built with an Amphenol 225-22821-110 (0.156" pitch, 2 × 28) whose opposing contacts touch when empty; the common 225-22821-101 does not, so -110 may be the normalling variant, though no datasheet surfaced. Surplus 225-series parts run $30–50 each. For the custom coupler: just letting the bumps touch gives a 2 gf "short" and 96 % of yield at 1.70 mm; a real 10–15 gf normalled path costs about 1.5 mm of height (7–7.5 mm beams), and the patents' third-spring approach is the only way to get 20 gf without paying in height. In the kiss slot an empty end would join a card's front pad n to its own back pad n, which is a loop-back and not a pass-through; pass-through-when-empty belongs on a single-slot connector on the main board.

    Not card edge, for reference

    From the market survey (Oct 2026, LCSC / DigiKey / Alibaba listings): a 70-way link done with an FFC and two 0.5 mm flip-lock ZIFs costs about $0.50–0.70 (XUNPU FPC-05FB-60PH20 $0.20 at 1.5 k, 60-way 50 mm FFC $0.10 at 5 k; ZIFs are rated for about 20 mating cycles); a 1.27 mm 2 × 40 header pair about $0.5–0.8; a gold-wire zebra strip plus clamp $0.05–1 for the strip (500-actuation spec); discrete Chinese pogo pins $0.10–0.28 each at 1 k, so $7 or more per 70 contacts; Mill-Max pogos $0.56–0.92 each. Resin in an LCP housing is about $0.02; gold on 70 selectively plated contacts about $0.2–0.4; a 2-cavity housing tool in China $8–15 k.
