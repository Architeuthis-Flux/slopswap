Edge connector options for V6 modules

Every way we have found to join a Jumperless module to the main board along an edge, with what each one costs in board area, height and money — one 70-way connector, or two or three smaller ones so half a breadboard can be swapped for something else. Includes the custom "kiss slot" coupler, the shorted-when-empty (normalling) type, and the off-LCSC Chinese parts. Numbers are from datasheets and listings as of 22 Nov 2026; custom-part numbers are estimates from the connector physics write-ups.

"Dead area" is the strip of PCB you cannot use for anything else: on a coplanar joint it is connector length × (host pad depth + air gap between the boards + module tongue); on a vertical or stacked joint it is the connector's footprint on the host plus the module's tongue. The options, one by one

    The custom "kiss slot" coupler

    The custom part designed for V6 (write-ups: V6_edge_coupler_connector_design and V6_edge_coupler_v2_kiss_slot in the project). Same 1.27 mm, 70-way, 45.47 mm tongue as today's board, but half the height, a third of the depth and the two boards touch inside the housing. The same contact strip in a shorter housing gives the 2-way and 3-way versions in the table above.

    Models: all five variants side by side (STEP, 7 MB zip) — today's WingTAT with cards, the 2.4 mm-web version, the kiss slot, a 7.8 mm short kiss slot and the 0.8 mm sister housing. Estimated cost at 10 k/yr: $2.7–4.2 in BeCu, $1.5–2.6 in phosphor bronze (which needs ~0.5 mm more height), against $2.15 for the WingTAT; tooling $13–25 k. The point is size and the touching boards, not price.

    Shorted-when-empty ("normalling") connectors

    A card-edge connector whose two rows of contacts touch each other when the slot is empty and are spread onto the two faces of the card when one is inserted. It exists as a technique — telecom DSX patch bays call it normalling, the patents say make-before-break or switching contacts — but not as a current catalog part at any fine pitch. Everything that covered it has expired:

    US 4,087,151Magnetic Controls / ADC, 1977second "normalling" hump on each contact rests against its opposite number; the card touches the first hump before spreading the second (make-before-break)
    US 4,514,030Methode, 1983three contact points per beam, the innermost shorts to the opposing contact
    US 5,098,306Burndy, 1992card edge connector with switching contacts
    US 5,286,215ADC Telecommunications, 1992normally-closed arms plus separate normally-open entrance springs
    US 6,764,345Tyco, 2003dual shorting contacts in BeCu, mate-before-break

    The only card-edge part with evidence of doing it is vintage: a synth-diy thread about a Buchla 208 built with an Amphenol 225-22821-110 (0.156" pitch, 2 × 28) whose opposing contacts touch when empty; the common 225-22821-101 does not, so -110 may be the normalling variant — no datasheet surfaced. Surplus 225-series parts run $30–50 each. For the custom coupler: just letting the bumps touch gives a 2 gf "short" and 96 % of yield at 1.70 mm; a real 10–15 gf normalled path costs ~1.5 mm of height (7–7.5 mm beams), and the patents' third-spring approach is the only way to get 20 gf without paying in height. In the kiss slot an empty end would join a card's front pad n to its own back pad n — a loop-back, not a pass-through; pass-through-when-empty belongs on a single-slot connector on the main board.

    Not card edge, for reference

    From the market survey (Oct 2026, LCSC/DigiKey/Alibaba listings): a 70-way link done with an FFC and two 0.5 mm flip-lock ZIFs costs about $0.50–0.70 (XUNPU FPC-05FB-60PH20 $0.20 at 1.5 k, 60-way 50 mm FFC $0.10 at 5 k; ZIFs are rated for ~20 mating cycles); a 1.27 mm 2 × 40 header pair about $0.5–0.8; a gold-wire zebra strip plus clamp $0.05–1 for the strip (500-actuation spec); discrete Chinese pogo pins $0.10–0.28 each at 1 k, so $7+ per 70 contacts; Mill-Max pogos $0.56–0.92 each. Branded 1.0/1.27 mm card-edge sockets (Samtec MEC1 80-pos, TE) are $3.3–6.3 at 1 k. Resin in an LCP housing is ~$0.02; gold metal on 70 selectively plated contacts ~$0.2–0.4; a 2-cavity housing tool in China $8–15 k.
