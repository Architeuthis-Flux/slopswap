Edge connector options for V6 modules

Every way found so far to join a Jumperless module to the main board along an edge, with what each costs in board area, height and money: one 70-way connector, or two or three smaller ones so half a breadboard can be swapped for something else. Catalog parts, Chinese and Taiwanese makers that are not (or barely) on LCSC, the custom "kiss slot" coupler, the single-contact SMT plug-and-socket clips, and the shorted-when-empty type. Dimensions are read from the makers' drawings and prices from listings as of 22 Nov 2026; anything marked ≈ is an estimate. Yuan prices are converted at ¥7.2 to the dollar.

Dead area is PCB that can hold nothing else. On a coplanar joint it is connector length × the whole strip from the back of the host pads to the tip of the module's tongue (host depth + board gap + tongue). On a vertical or raised joint it is the connector's footprint on the host plus the module's tongue. Host depth is solder legs on a straddle part, body plus about 1.8 mm of SMT leads on a vertical one, and body plus tails on a right-angle one. Gap is the distance from the host's edge to the tip of the card on a coplanar joint; tongue is the depth of the module the connector takes. Z height is how far the part stands off the board: total thickness for a coplanar part. Edge used allows 2 mm between connectors and turns orange when it exceeds the edge available. The signal count is a minimum: a connector with more contacts than asked for is fine and its extras are listed as spare, and edge holds is the most contacts that option could put on the edge with that many connectors. Depth reads host + gap + tongue for a coplanar part and host + tongue otherwise. A fixed-size part (PCIe, M.2) uses as many connectors as the signal count needs. Price is the listed price of the nearest size, scaled by contacts0.75 to the size used. What the numbers say

  • At 1.27 mm the edge will not split. Two 36-way housings need 59.9 mm and three 24-ways 68 mm, against the 50.5 mm one 70-way uses, because every housing brings its own 7.4 mm of end walls. A custom housing with one divider wall should carry 2 × 34 contacts in the same 50.5 mm (an estimate: 1.2 mm walls, today's 2.3 mm tongue margin).
  • Splitting with stock parts means 0.8 mm or finer. Two 40-ways at 0.8 mm take 43–50 mm (Samtec HSEC8-DV, Oupiin 8206-2 and 8208-2, Solepin 61C1 at 2 × 36). Three only fit as HSEC8-DV 3 × 26 (49 mm) or Oupiin's 0.635 mm right-angle part.
  • Coplanar from stock means a straddle part: a 12–13.5 mm strip with the boards about 2 mm apart at best, against 18.3 mm and 6.4 mm today. Samtec MEC8-EM is the only one that fits twice on the edge (2 × 40 in 49.6 mm, about $7.80 the pair). The XKB PCIe straddle is $0.57 but its screw ears make it 34.8 mm long.
  • The kiss slot is still the only option with the boards touching and a strip under 10 mm, and it is 5 mm thick where the straddle parts are 7–9.
  • Cheapest per contact is M.2: 67 contacts for $0.18, if a 0.8 mm card lying parallel to the host at 0.5 A a contact is acceptable.

    Single-contact SMT plug and socket (the Keystone 6100 / MAC8 KO-KC style)

    A stamped tab soldered flat on one board slides into a stamped spring socket soldered flat on the other, joining two boards in the same plane with nothing but metal: about 1.2 mm tall, on one face only, with the boards almost touching. Keystone and MAC8 make the gold-plated originals. The Chinese LED-lighting trade makes the same thing in tin-plated phosphor copper for joining light-bar boards end to end, at roughly a tenth of the price. Both boards need a part soldered on, so the module is no longer a bare PCB, and at about 3 mm per contact a 51 mm edge holds 16 or 17 of them per face.

    Caveats. These are lighting parts: tin plating, no stated mating-cycle life, and tolerances of ±0.15 to ±0.30 mm on the stamped bodies. Only two distinct all-metal tool sets turned up (the 81013 and the Gd-I059). The recommended pad is 2.60 mm wide, so 3.0 mm is about the tightest pitch. Listed 1688 prices are for small lots and usually fall with quantity.

    The custom "kiss slot" coupler

    The custom part designed for V6 (write-ups: V6_edge_coupler_connector_design and V6_edge_coupler_v2_kiss_slot in the project). Same 1.27 mm, 70-way, 45.47 mm tongue as today's board, but half the length end to end, 5.0 mm thick instead of 8.9, and the two boards touch inside the housing. The same contact strip in a shorter housing gives the 2-way and 3-way versions in the table above.

    Models: all five variants side by side (STEP, 7 MB zip): today's WingTAT with cards, the 2.4 mm-web version, the kiss slot, a 7.8 mm short kiss slot and the 0.8 mm sister housing. Estimated cost at 10 k a year: $2.7–4.2 in BeCu, $1.5–2.6 in phosphor bronze (which needs about 0.5 mm more height), against $2.15 for the WingTAT; tooling $13–25 k. The point is size and the touching boards, not price.

    Shorted-when-empty ("normalling") connectors

    A card-edge connector whose two rows of contacts touch each other when the slot is empty and are spread onto the two faces of the card when one is inserted. It exists as a technique (telecom DSX patch bays call it normalling, the patents say make-before-break or switching contacts) but not as a current catalog part at any fine pitch. Everything that covered it has expired:

    US 4,087,151Magnetic Controls / ADC, 1977second "normalling" hump on each contact rests against its opposite number; the card touches the first hump before spreading the second (make-before-break)
    US 4,514,030Methode, 1983three contact points per beam, the innermost shorts to the opposing contact
    US 5,098,306Burndy, 1992card edge connector with switching contacts
    US 5,286,215ADC Telecommunications, 1992normally-closed arms plus separate normally-open entrance springs
    US 6,764,345Tyco, 2003dual shorting contacts in BeCu, mate-before-break

    The only card-edge part with evidence of doing it is vintage: a synth-diy thread about a Buchla 208 built with an Amphenol 225-22821-110 (0.156" pitch, 2 × 28) whose opposing contacts touch when empty; the common 225-22821-101 does not, so -110 may be the normalling variant, though no datasheet surfaced. Surplus 225-series parts run $30–50 each. For the custom coupler: just letting the bumps touch gives a 2 gf "short" and 96 % of yield at 1.70 mm; a real 10–15 gf normalled path costs about 1.5 mm of height (7–7.5 mm beams), and the patents' third-spring approach is the only way to get 20 gf without paying in height. In the kiss slot an empty end would join a card's front pad n to its own back pad n, which is a loop-back and not a pass-through; pass-through-when-empty belongs on a single-slot connector on the main board.

    Not card edge, for reference

    From the market survey (Oct 2026, LCSC / DigiKey / Alibaba listings): a 70-way link done with an FFC and two 0.5 mm flip-lock ZIFs costs about $0.50–0.70 (XUNPU FPC-05FB-60PH20 $0.20 at 1.5 k, 60-way 50 mm FFC $0.10 at 5 k; ZIFs are rated for about 20 mating cycles); a 1.27 mm 2 × 40 header pair about $0.5–0.8; a gold-wire zebra strip plus clamp $0.05–1 for the strip (500-actuation spec); discrete Chinese pogo pins $0.10–0.28 each at 1 k, so $7 or more per 70 contacts; Mill-Max pogos $0.56–0.92 each. Resin in an LCP housing is about $0.02; gold on 70 selectively plated contacts about $0.2–0.4; a 2-cavity housing tool in China $8–15 k. Also looked at and set aside: Amphenol Mini Cool Edge (0.6 mm, caged, $3.01 at 2.8 k, 15-week lead), Hirose FX27 (floating, 22 mm tall), Samtec MEC5-RA (11.4 mm deep).
