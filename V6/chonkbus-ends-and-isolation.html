0. Bottom line

  • Two identical buses, same option list each. That is the mental model: "there are two 0.1 Ω ChonkBuses; any row can be on either; each bus can be TOP_RAIL, BOTTOM_RAIL, GND, DAC 0 or DAC 1 (one of these), plus the extension port, plus a shunt ammeter to the other bus." Making one bus GND-only would have saved nothing (the decoder makes exclusivity free) and cost the two-positive-supplies case.
  • Hardware interlocks only where no override could ever be wanted; firmware everywhere else. A 74HC238 per bus turns 3 latch bits + 1 enable into at most one voltage source, so no latch content, bit-order bug or half-shifted chain can put two sources on one bus. Two single gates stop the shunt from closing while both buses carry a source. Everything else (bus = rail and extension port, a row on both buses, shunt with one sourced bus) is a plain latch bit, policed by a low-level firmware "bus manager" with an override flag.
  • Break-before-make is inherent but not guaranteed: with the decoder the old switch opens (tOFF 5 µs typ) before the new one closes (tON 15 µs typ); the datasheet gives no maxima, so the firmware sequence is enable low → latch → new code + enable high → latch (two chain updates, ~20 µs apart).
  • Row isolation: FITTED (your call later on 2026-10-29; the first install had it as footprints with 0R bridges). It costs +$4.47 in parts at 1k (30 × ET7460H $3.60, 8 × 74HC595 $0.49, 38 × 100 nF $0.38) plus ~16 net placements, i.e. about +6 % of parts, +3.5 % of unit cost; the 60 × 0R are now the option (OPTION ROW-BRIDGE, dnp) for going back. Decide on the two bench tests (§4); the case for it is better than "–13/+18 V on idle rows" alone.
  • BOM as it stands (1000 boards, parts only, from the schematic, ROW-ISO fitted): main board $60.64, wig $18.62, total $79.26 (+ ~$2 of unpriced small parts; the display is in at its $8.49 sample AMOLED price — the TFT decision takes ~$5 off). Rough unit cost ex-works with assembly, mechanics, PCBs and packaging: ~$128 at 1k (SLS shell), ~$116 at 3k (moulded). §5 has the breakdown (pre-ISO numbers) and the caveats; the interactive version is the Cost Explorer.

Totals for the whole block: 67 ET7460H fitted (+3 vs the old option B), 17 × 74HC595, same CH32 pins (PF0/1/5/11/14) and the same chain length (136 bits).

Shunt numbers: INA219 LSB 10 µV → 100 µA per LSB on 0.1 Ω, ±3.2 A full scale at PGA /8, ~0.3 Ω in the path (two switches + shunt). Current A→B reads positive; the INA219 bus-voltage register gives V(BUS_B). Uses: row on A + row on B + shunt = a 0.3 Ω ammeter in series with anything; BUS_A = TOP_RAIL, the circuit's supply rows on BUS_B, shunt closed = supply current with a software fuse (open A_n/B_n on overcurrent, 5 µs). Limit: the INA219 inputs take −0.3…+26 V, so the shunt may only be closed while both buses are at or above 0 V (GND, TOP_RAIL or a DAC set ≥ 0) — the same limit V5's ISENSE has. With both shunt switches open the INA219 floats on its own nodes (internal ~830 kΩ to GND), so a negative bus never reaches it. The limit goes away with a $0.15 INA181-footprint amplifier (3PEAK TP181 / Gainsil GS199 / TI INA199) run with −9V as its ground and REF at +1.65 V (window −9.3…+27 V absolute; INA296A1 at $1.69 reaches −14 V), or with a ground-referenced ST TSC2011 ($1.33, −20…+70 V); output through the 8:1 housekeeping mux into one CH32 ADC channel, with two dividers for an always-on bus voltage read — V6_expansion_port_and_current_sense §1.5–1.6 (2026-10-30); not drawn yet.

Latch bit map (chip 1 = nearest SW_SER): chips 1–15 A_1 B_1 … A_60 B_60; chip 16 SRC_A_S0 S1 S2 EN SRC_B_S0 S1 S2 EN; chip 17 (aux) XB2_SEL0..2 EXT_A EXT_B SHUNT + 2 test points; chips 18–25 (ROW-ISO, fitted) ISO_1..ISO_60 + 4 spare: 200 bits. SW_SER_EXT to the extension board comes through solder jumper JP29001: A (bridged) = U29127 QH′ (chain end), B = aux QH′ (for the ROW-BRIDGE option, 136 bits). 100 kΩ pull-downs on every logic input the chain feeds (decoder address/enable, SHUNT, XB2_SEL0..2) so nothing floats while the latch outputs are Hi-Z at reset: every source off, shunt open, every switch open.

2. Hardware vs firmware: the interlock decision

What goes wrong without any interlock: two sources on one bus (TOP_RAIL and GND, say) is the L272D short-circuit current through 0.2 Ω; the TPS65131 delivers ~0.6–0.8 A at ±9 V before it folds, so the crossbar rails sag, every CH446Q may lose its routing, and the ET7460H (0.8 A continuous) cycles on thermal shutdown. On V5 the equivalent fault runs through 130 Ω of crosspoints and is benign; here it is not. A firmware-only rule would stop the normal case but not a crashed CH32, a corrupted SPI transfer into the chain, or a bit-order mistake while the firmware is young.

So the split is:

In hardware (no override possible) In firmware (bus manager, override flag)
≤ 1 voltage source per bus (74HC238; code 0 = none; enable = second lock and the break-before-make lever) bus = source and extension port (your case: an extension board powered from the rail)
shunt cannot close while both buses are sourced (NAND + AND, $0.11) extension port alone (external supply onto the bus, with the member rows isolated if it exceeds the crossbar range)
reset state = everything open (595 outputs Hi-Z + ET7460H 600 kΩ pull-downs + 100 kΩ pull-downs) a row closed on both buses (bridges them through 0.2 Ω; never by default)
shunt with one sourced bus, both buses ≥ 0 V (INA219 limit)
source change sequence: SRC_x_EN low, latch, new code + EN high, latch
ISO_n closed for every row the matrix routes or senses (option fitted)

The decoder costs $0.40 per board for two and saves two latch bits against five direct source bits per bus; the gates cost $0.11. The rejected alternatives: a fully firmware chain (cheapest, one brown-out away from a bad day), and asymmetric buses (one GND-only) which block two-positive-rail circuits (3V3 logic + 5 V peripheral) for no saving.

3. Row isolation: what the $4.47 buys

Sheet b29_isolation, fitted: one ET7460H switch per row between the breadboard row n and the crossbar side MX_ROW_n (CH446Q Y pin, lane-layer X port, BAT54S clamp), one package per two rows, 60 control bits. To go back (OPTION ROW-BRIDGE): fit the 60 × 0R 0402, leave the 30 switches, 8 latches and 38 capacitors out, move JP29001 to B (136-bit chain). Because the clamps and the lane layer sit on MX_ROW_n, the crossbar side was renamed on your sheet (60 labels at the CH446Q Y pins) so that the option is a pure fit/no-fit choice with no rewiring.

Without (bridges) With ROW-ISO
Idle row CH446Q Y port + BAT54S clamp: anything beyond the crossbar rails (±9.3 V, or +14.3/−4.3 V in the other range) is clamped; a stiff 12 V supply burns the 200 mA BAT54S, then the CH446Q (no series R in the row path, by decision) three open ET7460H, nothing else: −13/+18 V recommended, −15/+20 V abs max, no clamp current at all
Row in use as V5 as V5 (ISO closed; clamp active)
Bus from the extension port at a voltage the CH446Q cannot take (12 V, −12 V) not possible (member rows expose the matrix) possible with the member rows isolated: V6 can distribute an external supply to the breadboard
Jumperless unpowered, circuit externally powered rows clamped at ±0.3 V into dead rails: breadboard unusable works as a plain breadboard if the ET7460H blocks with VCC = 0 (bench test; the datasheet is silent, the AW35331 states it)
Reset / "rows completely passive" rule row always on its Y port row attached to nothing until the firmware closes ISO_n — the rule holds by construction
Per row path — +0.1 Ω, +~40 pF, +≤0.35 µA leakage (85 °C max; nA typical), one more control bit; the lane layer cannot look at an isolated row (by design)
Cost — +$4.47 parts (30 × $0.12 + 8 × $0.061 + 38 × $0.01; the 0R are $0 at Elecrow), ~16 net placements (~$0.25), 30 more WLCSP-12 to route, +5 mA quiescent, chain 136 → 200 bits (20 µs at 10 MHz)

It is fitted; run the two tests that decide whether it stays — (1) S/D blocking with VCC = 0 at −5/+8 V and at −12/+12 V, (2) off-capacitance and leakage of a row with three open switches — and keep it if (1) passes. If (1) fails, the feature shrinks to "idle rows take 18 V while the Jumperless is on" plus the external-supply bus; I would still keep it, but that is the point where the $4.47 is a judgement call rather than obvious.

4. Open items and bench list

  • ET7460H: Ron outside ±VCC (spec only inside), VCC = 0 blocking, off capacitance (60 open switches on a bus; 3 on an isolated row), charge injection, thermal-shutdown cycling into a short, EN tied to VCC on all packages (OPTION ROWSW-EN from the old note still applies if you want one-pin shutdown).
  • DAC 0 / DAC 1 as sources and DAC 0 taken at R_SENSE+: a proposal; you have not confirmed either. U4 (INA219 on R57) becomes redundant once the bus shunt exists (−$0.47).
  • INA219 common-mode limit (−0.3 V): replace it with a floated TP181-class amplifier (see the later note, §1.5); the 470 Ω trick from V5 does not transfer to a 0.1 Ω shunt.
  • Part numbers from LCSC search (not re-read datasheets): 74HC238PW,118 C60434 ($0.20@500/$0.18@2.5k), 74LVC1G00GW C12508 ($0.079@500), 74LVC1G08GW C12512 (~$0.03); the 1G00/1G08 pinout is the stock KiCad symbol (JEDEC single-gate). 0R1 1206 shunt: no part chosen ($0.03 estimate).
  • 100 kΩ pull-downs: value not from a source (74HC input leakage 1 µA → 0.1 V); the old 10 kΩ on XB2_SEL0..2 became 100 kΩ.
  • Extension connector pins 25–28 are now BUS_A_EXT/BUS_B_EXT (behind the EXT switches); pad current rating still unknown (B33). The ExtensionBoard project follows (its cells are on those nets).
  • Firmware: MatrixState/router must treat bus membership as a net attribute and (with ROW-ISO) close ISO_n on every routed or sensed row; the chain gains the source codes (3 bits + EN per bus) in place of the four per-source bits.

6. Names

ChonkBus, PowerBussy (yours); ChonkRail, FatBus, Beefy Bus, HardBus, Power Lane, HeavyLine. "ChonkBus" is the one that is both descriptive (chunky, bus) and says what it is in the UI ("put row 7 on ChonkBus A"); the latch/net names in the schematic stay BUS_A/BUS_B so nothing depends on the final pick.
